An e-bike subscription that had to make swapping a battery feel as simple as tapping a card.

Riders care about one thing: getting home. So the app is one ring of charge, and everything else waits.

The brief

Corso rents e-bikes by the month in Lisbon. Riders swap batteries at street hubs, and the old app split that into three separate flows. Battery questions made up 40% of all support calls.

Five more cities were planned within a year, each with its own engineers. Without a shared system every launch would mean rebuilding the app from scratch.

The idea

The battery is the hero of the interface: one ring showing charge, range and the nearest hub with a free slot. Swapping is a single screen. Billing, plans and settings all wait behind it.

We built the design system in Figma and in code at the same time, so the tokens engineers ship are the ones designers draw with.

What we made

  • Product strategy and UX
  • App UI for riders
  • Design system: 64 components, Figma and code
  • Identity refresh
  • Hub signage and bike decals

Designed for the handlebar

Thirty-eight riders tested prototypes on their own commutes. Every state was checked in direct sun on a handlebar mount, at arm’s length, with gloves on.
